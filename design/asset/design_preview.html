LifeOS · Design System

Grimoire

Shounen fantasy ink. Dark cover chrome, light parchment pages. This page is the lock-in artefact for Day 1 — open it, look at it on the real projector, and commit to the type stack. Every colour below is measured, not eyeballed.

The dark bands top and bottom are the only dark chrome in the system. The readout in the bottom bar measures what share of the screen they actually occupy — the hard ceiling is <= 15%, because a lit-room projector lifts black levels and crushes dark regions into a single muddy mass exactly when the panel is looking at them.

02 — Contrast Audit

Measured, not eyeballed

Computed live in this page against WCAG 2.1 relative luminance. The target is AAA (7:1), not AA — a projector burns roughly half the contrast away, so a 7:1 pair lands near 4.5:1 on the wall and is still readable. A 4.5:1 pair does not.

PairSampleRatioVerdictPermitted use

The PROBE and REJECTED rows are design options that were tried and measured, kept here on purpose so the decisions can be re-checked rather than re-argued. Two of them changed the system:

  • Dark text on the cinnabar accent fails at 2.58:1. The intuitive choice — a deep red fill with near-black text — is the one that does not work. Accent fills carry light text, and accent.deep is the variant to reach for when a filled element must clear 7:1.
  • Only the boss chip can be filled. Against light label text, easy, medium and hard land at 5.24, 5.83 and 5.67 — AAA-large, none of them reaching 7:1. Boss plum lands at 8.91:1 and clears AAA outright. So easy, medium and hard are outlined chips with ink text at 12.69:1 and the hue carried by a 3px border and a marker square, while boss is the one solid filled chip. Boss ends up different in kind, not just in colour — which is what the design asked for in the first place.

Hatching — decorative surfaces only

The map room

Diagonal hatch is the genre’s shading grammar, and it lives only on large decorative panels. Behind body copy it collapses into a grey smear at projection distance — which is why the note you are reading sits on a solid parchment plate.

05 — Presentation Mode

The highest-leverage feature here

The button in the top bar sets one attribute on <html>. That single attribute multiplies the type scale by 1.4, drops the grain overlay and switches off every animation. It is the difference between hoping the projector is bright enough and knowing the text is readable.

Toggled on

Body copy goes 17px → ~24px, which is the projector floor for content that has to be read from three metres. Grain is removed — at 4% opacity nobody in the room can see it, and it is the most expensive effect in the system.

Also wired to

prefers-reduced-motion and prefers-contrast: more. Users who need it get it without pressing anything, and the Motion Off button in the bar lets you rehearse that state before the demo.

06 — Rules

What this system forbids

Never

  • #FFFFFF as a content surface — glare in the dark, halation on the projector
  • Soft or blurred shadows. Every shadow is a hard offset, 0 blur, integer pixels
  • Uniform border-radius across components
  • Grenze Gotisch outside the logo, chapter titles and level-up
  • A live feTurbulence filter anywhere
  • Fine crosshatch behind body text
  • Mana rendered as a gradient — it must snap at the threshold
  • Hardcoded colours. Everything routes through a token

Always

  • Body text at ink.900 or ink.700 — both measured AAA against the page
  • Semantic strokes at 2px minimum, 3px preferred
  • Press feedback as movement into the shadow, not a colour change
  • Dark chrome under 15% of screen area
  • Loading, empty and error states drawn by hand, not library defaults
  • Accent fills carrying light text — never dark
  • Easy / medium / hard as outlined chips with ink text; boss as the one filled chip
  • Cover chrome text at cover.fg (13.96:1), not fgMuted, below 18px
